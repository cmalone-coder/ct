## April 6–9, 2004 — Free Groin Flap to Right Face (age 14)

The foundational reconstructive procedure, carried out as planned from the December 2003 consultation. A multi-day inpatient admission with two coordinated operative teams: **[flap surgeon]** harvested a free flap from the right groin, and **[surgeon]** (Dr. Ha) performed the facial recipient-site work.

**Diagnosis:** Right facial hemi-macrosomia (right-sided Romberg disease), with significant right malar deficiency and cheek soft-tissue atrophy causing facial asymmetry.

**Procedure — flap harvest ([flap surgeon]):** The dominant flap artery was identified by Doppler roughly 3 cm below the inguinal crease. A flap matching a pre-marked facial skin template (~6 cm at greatest width) was elevated in the subcutaneous and deeper planes with careful vessel preservation, then transferred to the facial region on a cool saline pad for microvascular anastomosis. The donor site was closed with transposition flaps from the anterior abdomen and thigh, over a 9-mm Jackson-Pratt drain.

**Procedure — facial recipient site ([surgeon]):** Via an upper gingivobuccal incision, the anterior maxilla was exposed (infraorbital nerve identified and preserved), and 5cc of hydroxyapatite mixed with the patient's own blood was packed onto the maxillary skeletal tissue for augmentation. The flap was then anastomosed to the facial vessels (9-0/10-0 suture) with excellent inflow confirmed. Estimated blood loss 200cc; no complications.

**Hospital course:** Admitted to the ICU postoperatively for flap monitoring — excellent signal, flap viable throughout. Transferred to the floor on postop day 1. Slow to advance diet (soft diet + nutritional supplementation recommended), ambulating by postop day 3. Discharged home the night of April 8, 2004 on Keflex, Lortab, and Bacitracin wound care, with follow-up with [surgeon] on April 12, 2004.

## November 19–20, 2007 — LeFort I Osteotomy for Distraction (age 18)

**Diagnosis:** Romberg syndrome, maxillary hypoplasia.

**History:** Seen in consultation with **[another surgeon]** (Dr. Sinn); after examination and imaging review, consented for a right posterior maxillary LeFort I osteotomy with placement of a KLS-Martin distraction device for gradual distraction osteogenesis.

**Procedure:** A standard LeFort-type incision was made in the right maxillary vestibule. During dissection, a previous hydroxyapatite graft (from the 2004 procedure) was found still partially present and loosely bound to the maxilla; it was removed. The right upper canine (tooth #6) was found still impacted, fenestrated through the maxillary bone — it was left in place for possible future orthodontic eruption, with the osteotomy placed just posterior to it. The osteotomy was completed with a reciprocating saw and osteotomes, the segment mobilized, and a 12-mm KLS-Martin distraction device fixated with cortical screws.

**Hospital course:** Uneventful overnight admission (23-hour observation), discharged the next day tolerating a soft mechanical diet with pain well controlled. A minor "gum incident" was noted and reported to on-call staff overnight with no new orders required. Discharge medications: Afrin, Amoxicillin, Sudafed, Peridex rinse.

## January 28–29, 2008 — Right Hemi-LeFort I Osteotomy, Hardware Removal & Extraction (age 18)

**Diagnosis:** Romberg's syndrome, maxillary asymmetry.

**History:** The patient presented with persistent (though improved) asymmetry following the 2007 distraction, with the dentoalveolar segment still medially displaced and above the occlusal plane. Surgeon **[another surgeon]** (Dr. Sinn), assistant **[assistant surgeon]**.

**Procedure:** Via a right vestibular LeFort-type incision, the distraction device was removed — over 1cm of good bone had formed at the distraction site, but the dentoalveolar segment remained malpositioned. Osteotomes were used to create a new osteotomy at the impacted upper canine (tooth #6), which was extracted. Intermaxillary fixation with 26-gauge wire brought the segment into proper occlusion, secured with an S-shaped titanium plate. This left an ~8mm step-off at the maxillary segment, which was grafted using a shaped hydroxyapatite block.

**Hospital course:** Admitted overnight for observation, discharged home the following day tolerating oral intake well, pain controlled, ambulating without issue. Discharge medications: Lortab, Peridex. Follow-up with Dr. Sinn in 1–3 weeks.
